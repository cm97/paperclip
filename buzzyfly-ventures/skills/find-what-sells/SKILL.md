# Find what sells

Rule one: **build what people already buy.** A competitor making money is
good news. It proves demand. We win by being more focused, simpler, cheaper,
or aimed at a buyer the big players ignore.

## The patterns behind small businesses that reach $100k/month

These come from public, mostly founder-reported case studies (treat the
numbers as directional, not guaranteed):

1. **One painful job, done well.** The winners solve one narrow task (chat
   with a PDF, collect testimonials, schedule social posts), not "everything".
2. **Free tool → paid upgrade.** A useful free tier brings visitors from
   search and word of mouth. The paid tier removes a limit.
3. **Distribution before product.** The founder already had, or deliberately
   built, a way to reach buyers: search pages, a community, an audience, a
   marketplace listing.
4. **Sell to people who spend other people's money.** Teachers, schools,
   managers and small businesses buy faster and pay more than individuals.
5. **Portfolios beat bets.** Many successful solo founders ran several small
   products and kept the ones that sold.
6. **It takes time.** Verified data shows most small software products stay
   under $1k/month and the typical path to $100k/month takes years. Plan for
   many small wins, not one overnight hit.

## Scorecard (score 0–3 each, build only if total ≥ 15 of 21)

| Factor | 0 | 3 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Proof of payment | none found | several sellers with reviews/sales |
| Pain | nice to have | costs the buyer time, money or grades now |
| Free reach | no free channel | clear search terms + communities + direct buyers |
| Buyer budget | broke individuals | teachers/schools/businesses |
| Build size | months | one Worker in a week |
| Repeat or upsell | one-off | ongoing use, classroom/team tier |
| Fit with Buzzyfly | unrelated | shares buyers or code with existing apps |

## Starting shortlist (to verify, not assumed)

Categories that keep showing up as bestsellers on digital marketplaces:
planners (ADHD, homeschool, study), teacher classroom tools, budgeting and
expense trackers, small-business templates (invoices, client onboarding,
contracts), resume and job-search helpers, wedding and event planning tools.
Turn each into a **web app**, not a download, so it can't be copied and resold.

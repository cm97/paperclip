# The 25 No's system

The owner's rule: **go looking for 25 no's.** A "no" isn't failure. It's
data. Around 25 no's usually comes with a yes, and each batch of 25 shows what
to fix before the next round.

## Rules

1. **25 different people.** A no from one person counts once. Never contact
   someone again after they say no or ask you to stop (that breaks CAN-SPAM
   and gets our email blocked). Add them to the do-not-contact list.
2. **Ask for a clear answer.** End every pitch with a yes/no question
   ("Would this help your class before finals?"). Silence after one follow-up
   counts as "no reply", not a no.
3. **Log every no with the reason, in their words.**
4. **At 25 no's, stop and review.** Group the reasons. Pick the biggest one.
   Change **one thing** (the audience, the offer, the price, the message, the
   channel or the product), then start the next 25.
5. **Celebrate the yes, then find out why** they said yes, and do more of that.

## The review at 25 no's

Write a short note to the Head of Growth and the CEO:

- The top 3 reasons for no, with counts.
- What the yeses had in common.
- The one change for the next round, and why.
- Whether to keep this channel or audience.

**Check the product first.** Many no's mean the product has a gap: something
missing, broken or that looks untrustworthy. Before changing the pitch, ask:
did they try it? Did checkout, unlock and support work? Did anything feel
unclear or shady? Send product gaps to the Builder as bugs, fixed before the
next round.

Common fixes: "too expensive" → lead with the free tier or a smaller tier.
"Already use X" → show the one thing we do better, or pick a different audience.
"Not now" → time it before exams or budget season. "Not my decision" →
ask who decides and reach that role instead.
